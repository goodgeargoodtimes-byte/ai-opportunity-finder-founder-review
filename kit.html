Your Business Context Prompt

Paste this at the start of a new AI conversation before using the task prompts in this kit.

Master contextCopy / paste
I operate a small Australian trade-service business and I mainly use Microsoft 365. My current priority is responding to customer enquiries faster and more consistently. When helping me draft or analyse customer communications: - use only facts I provide; - never invent pricing, availability, scope, technical findings, timeframes or commitments; - if important information is missing, identify it rather than making an assumption; - keep customer-facing wording clear, practical and professional; - flag anything that requires my judgement before it is sent; - do not treat AI output as final approval. If the task involves sensitive, confidential or customer information, remind me to use an appropriate approved system rather than pasting it into a public AI tool.

Your tailored prompt library

Use these as working prompts, not as instructions to auto-send customer messages. Review every output before use.

1. Draft a new-enquiry replyStart
Using the business context above, draft a concise reply to the enquiry below. Objectives: 1. Acknowledge the enquiry. 2. Answer only what can be answered from the information supplied. 3. Identify any missing information needed before the next step. 4. End with one clear next action. Do not invent price, timing, availability, scope or technical conclusions. ENQUIRY: [PASTE NON-SENSITIVE ENQUIRY]
2. Find the missing informationCheck
Review this customer enquiry and list only the information I still need before I can decide the next step. Separate essential information from useful-but-optional information. Do not answer the customer's question yet.
3. Classify the enquiryOrganise
Classify this enquiry as one of: new job, quote request, scheduling, existing-job follow-up, complaint/problem, general question or other. Explain the classification in one sentence and state what human decision is required next.
4. Make a reply shorter and clearerImprove
Rewrite this draft so it is shorter, clearer and easier for the customer to act on. Preserve every factual detail and do not add any new commitments or claims.
5. Check for invented factsQuality control
Compare this draft with the source information. List every statement that is not directly supported, is ambiguous, or appears to be an assumption. Do not rewrite until I approve the corrections.
6. Check commercial promisesRisk check
Review this customer-facing draft and flag any wording that could be interpreted as a promise about price, timing, availability, job acceptance, scope, warranty, refund or outcome. Suggest safer wording without weakening confirmed facts.
7. Create a follow-upRepeat
Draft a short, polite follow-up for this enquiry. Make it easy for the customer to either continue or close the loop. Do not imply urgency or pressure unless I specifically provide it.
8. Turn repeated enquiries into FAQsSystemise
Review these anonymised historical enquiry examples. Identify recurring questions and group them into themes. Draft an FAQ only from information that appears consistently in the source material and flag anything requiring my confirmation.
9. Build a reusable response templateSystemise
Using these three approved responses to the same type of enquiry, create one reusable template. Keep fixed wording separate from fields I need to customise each time.
10. Complaint acknowledgementHuman review required
Draft a brief acknowledgement of this complaint. Confirm that the concern has been received and will be reviewed. Do not admit liability, promise an outcome, invent facts or speculate about cause. Highlight any part that needs my direct judgement before sending.
11. Internal handover summaryOrganise
Turn this customer thread into an internal handover containing: customer request, confirmed facts, missing information, actions already taken, next action and any commitment already made.
12. Improve the prompt when results are poorTroubleshoot
Review your previous answer. Identify where it became generic, made assumptions or failed to use the context I supplied. Ask me for any missing information needed to improve the result, then produce a revised version using only confirmed facts.

Example: new enquiry template

Thanks for getting in touch.

To help me understand the job, please send through the location, a brief description of the issue, when you first noticed it, whether it is urgent and any useful photos.

Once I have that information, I can review the appropriate next step.

Human-review rules

Suitable for assisted drafting: routine acknowledgements, missing-information requests, standard follow-ups, note summaries and approved FAQ/template work.
Always review directly: prices, scope, technical judgement, complaints, refunds, commitments, legal issues, safety issues, unusual circumstances, sensitive information and anything consequential for the customer.
